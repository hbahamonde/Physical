# Ambiguity: peer-reviewed literature evidence and claim boundaries

Search conducted 12 October 2026. Scope: gendered candidate appearance, facial sex typicality, presentation nonconformity, category ambiguity, and electoral outcomes, with related peer-reviewed measurement and mechanism papers. This is a thorough bounded scoping search, not a registered systematic review or a claim to exhaustive bibliographic coverage. Searches began with the complete reference list of the supplied Bahamonde--Sarpila paper, then followed the closest studies, their references and citing studies, and targeted recent publications through October 2026. Only peer-reviewed journal articles provide substantive literature evidence below. News, dissertations, books, preprints, generic aggregator entries, and conference programmes were discovery aids or exclusions.

## Decisive assessment

The broad proposed gap -- gender-atypical/ambiguous candidate appearance and electoral success -- is already occupied. Hehman et al. (2014), Carpinella et al. (2016), Wojcik and Mullenax (2024), and Naunov (2026) must be acknowledged. Park and Joo (2023) also already obtain separate masculinity/femininity ratings of campaign photographs and relate their inferred traits to actual vote shares.

A defensible contribution is narrower: use independently elicited dimensions to compare **joint appearance configurations** in Finnish open-list municipal elections, distinguish a move toward opposite-gender coding from simultaneous high masculinity/high femininity and simultaneous low masculinity/low femininity, and test whether these configurations have different associations with personal vote counts within the same electoral setting. The claim is that this comparison adds a substantively different question and a measurement-sensitive test, not that no earlier study has measured two dimensions or analyzed real votes. Avoid a first-ever claim. The exact measurement in Wojcik--Mullenax remains unresolved because full text was inaccessible; its published abstract establishes close substantive overlap, and a draft should acknowledge that limitation rather than assert a verified method gap relative to this paper.

## Critical measurement caution in the existing data

The original questionnaire asks masculinity and femininity **compared with others of the same age and gender**, on separate 1--5 scales, with different random rater samples for each attribute (attached paper, Table 1 and pp.629--630). Therefore:

1. High masculinity for a woman means more masculine-looking relative to women of similar age; it does not imply that raters would classify that person as male. Similarly, equal M and F ratings do not establish perceptual gender uncertainty.
2. These measures are outsider impressions. They do not establish transgender or nonbinary identity, sexuality, self-identification, or deliberate self-presentation.
3. A pooled M--F difference treats scale units as comparable. With gender-relative anchors and independent raters, that is an analytic contrast requiring sensitivity checks, not a naturally calibrated ambiguity probability.
4. A pair of high scores and a pair of low scores have the same difference but carry different information. Keep the dimensions in the model. Compare supported observed percentiles/profile contrasts, and do not extrapolate to nonexistent high/high corners.
5. Gender-stratified percentiles or standardized dimensions can describe within-group relative profiles but change the estimand. Report the operational choice explicitly, and retain raw-scale robustness.
6. Municipality and party indicators adjust shared context, not candidate-level incumbency/campaign resources, list-specific strength, or all voter information. These are associations; direct mechanisms require future validation/experiment.

## Recommended theory-to-test bridge

Use Monk to ask how inequality is allocated within recorded gender categories. Existing electoral evidence establishes that gender typicality matters but does not support a universally negative effect of departures. The primary contrast describes a targeted, gender-relative direction of cue variation; the secondary party model examines whether its electoral association varies across political contexts. Joint cue level and nonlinear configurations remain additional appendix specifications. Compare model contrasts and their uncertainty; these data do not identify warmth, dominance, categorical ambiguity, or voter-ideology mechanisms.

Suggested contribution sentence: **We connect infracategorical inequality to electoral support by examining a gender-relative contrast of independently elicited femininity and masculinity ratings. Finnish personal-vote returns permit comparisons within gender categories and party contexts, conditional on other appearance ratings and candidate attributes.**

That sentence describes the project's actual contribution without asserting an unverified absence of all prior configuration tests.

## Trait inferences and leadership fit: focused follow-up, 13 October 2026

- Walker and Wanke (2017), PLOS ONE 12(10):e0181306, DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0181306. Full published paper inspected. The abstract quotation is on PDF page 1. Manipulated femininity increased inferred warmth but reduced inferred competence; the target's gender remained unambiguous.
- Carpinella and Johnson (2013), Social Cognition 31(6):770-779, DOI 10.1521/soco.2013.31.6.770. Full published author copy inspected. The results quotation is on printed page 775/PDF page 6. The male femininity-warmth association concerns liberal politicians; it does not establish voting effects.
- Spisak, Dekker, Kruger and van Vugt (2012), PLOS ONE 7(1):e30399, DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0030399. Full published paper inspected. The results quotation is on PDF page 5. Mock-election preferences differed between conflict and peace contexts; the study did not measure personality-impression mediation.
- Laustsen and Petersen (2016), Political Communication 33(2):188-211, DOI 10.1080/10584609.2015.1050565. The exact quotation is verified in the published abstract and cited with an abstract locator. Audience-dependent dominance preferences supply contextual evidence; dominance is not the present femininity-masculinity contrast.
- Wang, Merolla and Manganiello (2023), Politics & Gender 19(4):1180-1203, DOI 10.1017/S1743923X23000314. Full published paper inspected, especially printed page 1195/PDF page 16. Communal male campaign language reduced overall warmth and likability without a significant vote-choice effect. Appearance, communal language, and perceived warmth are distinct constructs.

Interpretation: these studies motivate context-dependent leadership fit as a possible explanation of the male association. They do not establish that Finnish voters perceived cooperative leadership demands or that warmth mediates the cue-vote relationship. Party-specific estimates test observed organizational contexts, not individual voter ideology.
